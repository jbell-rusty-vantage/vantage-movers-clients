## State-page findings

The Florida page combines a location-specific hero and quote form, a provider/trust band, four reasons-to-choose cards, six broad service cards, city links, a compact set of Florida service links, four local challenge cards, arrival preparation, route links, testimonials, FAQs and a final quote action. Its location navigation exposes a short list of states and an all-states link.

The useful pattern is progressive detail: orient the visitor, collect their actual move details, explain logistics particular to that state, then offer an appropriate service or directional route. City names should help customers describe their move; they must not become links to nonexistent pages.

## Directional-route findings

The FL → NC page adds departure/arrival context, inventory and access preparation, broker/carrier responsibilities, timing and cost drivers, packing and arrival tips, official address-change links, FAQs, related routes and links back to state coverage. Its linked images include a route graphic. The supplied Owner brief additionally requires a dynamic map with two state pins and a connecting line; a markdown/HTML scrape alone does not establish how the competitor's map behaves interactively.

Useful route content distinguishes both ends: Florida building reservations and loading access; the actual North Carolina city, driveway and receiving conditions; key dates; optional packing/storage questions; and essentials traveling with the customer. A state-centroid connection is orientation, not a driving route, mileage estimate, transit promise or carrier-tracking display.

## Vantage adaptation

- Use Vantage's existing quote contract, broker identity and service catalog. The page supplies context; visitors still enter and confirm their own addresses and dates.
- Use original, source-backed local planning guidance. Do not inherit competitor ratings, discounts, shipment counts, coverage guarantees, app tracking or matching-algorithm claims.
- Crosslink actual reviewed/preview-available entries. Name the route section as planning routes rather than claiming “most requested” without Vantage demand evidence.
- Present official sources as useful reading cards, with freshness and review metadata retained in the editorial model. Consumer UI should not expose Codex authorship or workflow notes.
- Keep preview visibility separate from approval, production publication and indexing. Inclusion in this preview is authorized; publication remains a separate decision.
- Scale through typed state facts and independently authored directed-route overlays. Twenty states allow 380 directed interstate pairs, not 190 interchangeable routes. Neither the route matrix nor state-name substitution is an editorial approval mechanism.

The implementation and operating design accompany this research in this packet. Primary-source findings belong with the content source registry, not with competitor assertions.
